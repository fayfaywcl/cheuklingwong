Springbound

An educational physics game about using mass and spring energy to move a ball through a block-based course.

Timeline

Course prototype

Technologies

Project Overview

Springbound turns spring mechanics into a playable physics challenge. Players enter a mass value and use the resulting spring force and kinetic and potential energy to change a ball’s movement and pass through blocks.

View the Project on GitHub

Learning physics through interaction

This project explores how a physics concept can become something players manipulate and observe. The central relationship is between the spring constant, the mass entered by the player, and the ball’s motion. Rather than only presenting the idea as a formula, the game lets the player change a value and see its effect while trying to pass the blocks.

The design challenge is to connect the input, the physics response, and the obstacle course clearly enough that players can understand why the ball moves as it does. This makes the level a place to experiment with the relationship between stored spring energy, movement, and the path ahead.

From sketch to playable system

I began with a rough gameplay sketch to explore how the ball could move through a course. I then implemented the core interaction around the spring constant and kinetic and potential energy. The player can enter the mass and use the resulting physics to change the ball’s position and pass the blocks.

The playable system gives me a way to examine how the physics variables affect the experience and how well the course communicates those effects. I would be happy to walk through the gameplay and implementation during a project review.

The core experiment is simple: change the mass, observe the ball’s response, and use that motion to get through the course.
