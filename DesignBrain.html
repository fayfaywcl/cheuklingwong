Project Overview

Design Brain began from a simple problem: creative people collect inspiration everywhere, but the ideas, links, images, feelings, and reasons behind those ideas often become scattered across apps. The result is a loss of emotional context and a harder time returning to inspiration when a project needs momentum.

The project proposes a web platform that combines inspiration collection, project organization, process recording, music-supported focus, motivation features, and AI-assisted transformation from loose ideas into structured project content.

Target Audience

CART students and creative individuals who collect design inspiration

Problem Space

The core challenge was not just saving inspiration. It was saving why something mattered, when it was found, and how it could support the next design decision.

How might we

How might we design a platform that helps creative individuals collect, organize, and revisit design inspiration in a way that preserves both ideas and the emotional context behind them?

Creative inspiration Emotional context Project memory CART students Less scattered tools

Research

I focused the research on CART students because their work often crosses art, code, design, media, games, and interactive systems.

Interviews

Six CART Students

I asked about inspiration habits, current tools, emotional response, organization, pressure, deadlines, and what slows their process down.

Think-Aloud

Real Workflow Clues

Participants described how they use tools like Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, Apple Notes, Google Docs, Notion, Figma, and Blender.

Observation

Fragmented Notes

One observation showed multiple inspiration notes, reels links, bullet points, and project notes spread across Apple Notes and Google Docs.

Key Findings

The research moved the design away from a simple gallery and toward a tool for memory, process, and motivation.

Finding 01

Ideas Are Scattered

Students save references across many platforms, making it difficult to find an idea again when the project grows.

Finding 02

Reasons Matter

People wanted to record why they saved something, such as color combinations, mood, draft direction, or a specific design idea.

Finding 03

Stress Changes Searching

Deadlines can motivate, but they can also make inspiration searching feel pressured and time-consuming.

Finding 04

Keep It Simple

Feedback suggested that the platform should avoid over-complication, ads, and deep navigation that takes too many clicks.

Design Direction

The concept became a project-centered inspiration workspace with lightweight gamification and a calm creative mood.

01 Collect inspiration
02 Add reason, tags, and source
03 Move through process steps
04 Use AI to structure notes
05 Revisit progress and motivation

Feature System

The final feature direction supports inspiration capture, organization, focus, motivation, and AI-assisted project development.

Project Grid

Organized Inspiration

Projects are presented in a grid so users can quickly enter a design process, revisit references, and keep progress visible.

Scratchpad

Drag-and-Drop Thinking

The scratchpad encourages loose ideation without forcing everything into a polished structure too early.

AI Support

Convert Notes to Projects

The AI feature helps users transform scattered notes into more structured project content, while still allowing editing and refinement.

Motivation

Points and Rewards

Gamification uses points, badges, missions, and locked reward states to invite continued engagement without making it mandatory.

Music

Creative Mood

A music mini player and playlist area support focus, inspiration, and a more personal creative atmosphere.

Navigation

Breadcrumb Structure

Breadcrumbs communicate where users are inside a nested project hierarchy without making the interface visually crowded.

Affordance Thinking

I mapped interface actions through David's affordance lens to clarify what the product should invite, require, discourage, block, or enable.

Request

Create new project, scratchpad interaction, AI conversion, playlists, and points display invite action.

Demand

Progress navigation, templates, confirmation steps, and AI edit flow strongly guide user decisions.

Discourage

Delete warnings, locked states, spacing, and contrast fixes reduce mistakes and misinterpretation.

Refuse

Locked rewards, cancel options, and unavailable states block premature or unintended actions.

Allow

Breadcrumbs, profile systems, music controls, integrations, and hover effects make actions clear.
